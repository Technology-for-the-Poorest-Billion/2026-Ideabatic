# Future Work and Next Steps

The testing conducted so far has provided some insights into the causes of non-uniform cooling within the SMILE Go vaccine carrier. One of the most significant observations was the large temperature difference that develops during the first ten minutes of cooling, suggesting that the initial cooling phase could play a key role in creating temperature differences within the carousel. The results also largely ruled out door leakage as a major contributor to non-uniform cooling. Additionally, testing of the inserts with ice segmentation showed some slight improvements in cooling uniformity, indicating that changing the distribution of the ice pack may have an effect on temperature distribution within the carrier. However, a definitive root cause of the non-uniform cooling has not yet been determined, and more investigation is required. Testing also showed that improved insulation may be needed to ensure vaccines remain within the required temperature range throughout the cooling cycle.

## Further Testing

The next phase of the project will focus on experimental testing to investigate the remaining potential causes of non-uniform cooling. A key priority is the convection tests, which will involve introducing forced airflow using a fan to observe the influence of removing natural convection currents within the bottle. If convection is found to have an impact on temperature distribution, future design changes can be developed to mitigate this effect. Further experiments will investigate the effect of the ice pack making contact with only the lower section of the carousel, as this may create uneven heat transfer that contributes to temperature variation between vaccine locations.

## Analysis of Results

Following the completion of further testing, the data collected will be analysed to determine whether any observed changes are significant. This analysis will help determine a better understanding of the reasons for non-uniform cooling.

## Design Improvements

The outcomes of this testing will suggest future design improvements for the vaccine carrier. Once the main causes of non-uniform cooling have been identified, possible solutions can be suggested and evaluated. These could include changes to the carousel geometry, improvements to insulation, redesign of the ice pack arrangement, or the introduction of features intended to reduce temperature gradients within the system. The overall aim of further work is to improve cooling uniformity and ensure that all vaccine positions remain within the required storage temperature range throughout operation.
